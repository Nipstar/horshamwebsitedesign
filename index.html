A checklist to hold any site against

What a good Horsham business website includes

These six points decide most side-by-side comparisons. Use them to judge your current site, or any quote you are weighing up.

A first line that says what you do

Your trade and your town belong at the top of the page, in plain words. Visitors comparing three tabs will not scroll to work out whether you do the job they need.

A separate section for each service

Each service needs its own clear description, because a list of everything you do tells nobody whether you do their job well. Search engines and AI assistants read those sections too.

The villages you actually cover

Naming Southwater, Billingshurst or Henfield settles the first question a customer has, which is whether you come to them. A vague line about the surrounding area does not.

Proof from real work

Photos of finished jobs and reviews from named customers do more than any claim about quality. When two sites look alike, the one showing evidence is the one that gets trusted.

A price guide, even a rough one

A starting figure saves the customer a phone call just to find out if you are in budget. Firms that publish one look more straightforward than the ones that say nothing.

A quote form that takes a minute

Ask only for a name, a contact and the job, and keep the phone number one tap away on a mobile. Every extra box is another reason to go back to the other tab.
